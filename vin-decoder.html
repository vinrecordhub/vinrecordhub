What a free VIN decode tells you

Every VIN is a 17-character build code. Characters 1–8 identify the manufacturer, brand and vehicle attributes, character 9 is a checksum, character 10 is the model year, character 11 is the assembly plant, and the last six are the serial number. Decoding it reads that factory record back out.

  • Included free: model year, make, model, trim, body class, engine size and cylinders, fuel type, transmission, drive type, GVWR, manufacturer and assembly plant.

What a decode cannot tell you

Everything that happens after the car leaves the factory is an event record, reported later by insurers, repair shops, DMVs, auctions and police. None of it is encoded in the VIN, so no decoder — free or paid — can surface it.

  • Reported accidents and structural damage
  • Title brands — salvage, rebuilt, flood, lemon, junk
  • Odometer readings and rollback flags
  • Number of owners, and whether it was a rental, fleet or taxi
  • Service records, auction history and open liens

That gap is the whole reason history reports exist. A decode confirms the seller's listing is describing the right car; a report tells you whether it is worth the asking price.

How to use this before buying

Decode first. If the year, engine or trim does not match what the listing claims, you have caught a problem for free — either a typo or a misrepresentation, and both are worth knowing before you drive out to see it. Once the spec checks out, run the full history to see the car's actual past.

Frequently asked questions

Is this VIN decoder really free?

Yes. Decoding is free and unlimited, with no account and no email required. It reads the public NHTSA vPIC database, which publishes factory build data for every VIN sold in the United States.

What does a VIN decoder show?

A decoder reads the build specification encoded in the VIN itself: model year, make, model, trim, body class, engine, transmission, drive type and the plant that assembled the car. It does not show what happened to the car after it left the factory.

Does a free VIN decode show accidents or title problems?

No. Accidents, title brands such as salvage or flood, odometer readings and ownership history are event records collected after the sale. They are not encoded in the VIN and never appear in a free decode. A full vehicle history report is required, starting at $6.99.

Where do I find my VIN?

Look at the base of the windshield on the driver's side, on the driver's door jamb sticker, or on your title, registration and insurance card.

Why was my VIN rejected?

A VIN is exactly 17 characters and never contains the letters I, O or Q, which are excluded to avoid confusion with 1 and 0. Vehicles built before 1981 predate the standard and cannot be decoded.
