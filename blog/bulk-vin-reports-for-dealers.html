Bulk VIN Reports for Dealers & Importers

Running history checks on every unit that comes onto your lot — or every US-market car you're inspecting before export — shouldn't cost $40+ per VIN. Volume pricing gets you down to $4.00 a report, no subscription.

Why per-report pricing breaks down at volume

A single premium-brand vehicle history report retail-prices around $40–$45. That's a tolerable cost for a one-time private buyer. It's a real line item if you're running ten reports a week — whether you're a small used-car lot checking trade-ins, an independent flipper sourcing vehicles across multiple auctions, or a buyer inspecting US-titled vehicles before shipping them overseas. At retail pricing, ten reports a week is $400+ weekly, or roughly $1,700+ a month, before you've sold a single car.

VinRecordHub's credit bundles exist specifically to fix that math. You're not buying a dealer subscription with a monthly minimum — you buy a block of credits once, and each credit redeems for one report, Carfax® or AutoCheck® — your choice, per VIN.

No subscription — buy once, use on your schedule

Dealer-facing report subscriptions typically lock you into a monthly fee regardless of how many cars actually move through your lot that month. A slow month still bills you the same as a busy one. VinRecordHub's bundles work the opposite way: you pay once for a block of credits, and they sit on your account until you use them. Run five reports this week and none next week — the cost is identical to running them evenly spaced, because you already paid for the credits up front, once, with no recurring charge. There's no dealer license requirement to buy a bundle, and no minimum monthly usage to maintain a rate.

Pre-import inspection: US-history checks for Gulf-region buyers

A large share of used vehicles exported to Gulf-region markets originate in the US, often sourced through auctions, dealer trade-ins, or private sales before being shipped. Because the vehicle's accident, title and flood history typically lives in US databases — not in the destination country's registration system — the only practical way to check that history before the car leaves the US is to pull a US report on the VIN while it's still stateside.

This is a case where running both report brands on the same VIN earns its keep. Carfax® and AutoCheck® pull from different data pipelines (see our Carfax vs AutoCheck comparison for the detail), so a car that slipped through one provider's network without a flood or salvage flag might still show up in the other's. For a vehicle you're about to spend thousands of dollars shipping overseas, spending two credits — one Carfax, one AutoCheck — for cross-referenced coverage is inexpensive insurance against importing a car with hidden flood or accident damage that's much harder, and more expensive, to catch once it's landed.

How a small lot uses a bundle

A typical pattern for a small independent dealer: buy the 25-credit bundle at $99.99 ($4.00/report) at the start of the month. Run one report on every trade-in before making an offer, and one on every auction unit before bidding. When credits run low, buy another 25-pack — there's no expiration pressure forcing you to use them faster than your actual pace of business, and no penalty for buying a smaller 15-credit bundle instead if your volume is lighter some months.

What every report still includes

Bulk pricing doesn't mean a stripped-down report. Whether you redeem a credit for Carfax® or AutoCheck®, you get that provider's standard report — reported accidents and damage, title brands (salvage, rebuilt, flood, lemon, junk), odometer readings for rollback detection, and ownership/usage history where available. The only thing that changes at volume is the price per credit; the report itself is identical to what a single-purchase customer receives. That matters for a dealer's own due diligence trail — if a trade-in later turns out to have an undisclosed salvage title, having pulled a full report (not a summary) before the offer is what protects you.

Matching bundle size to your volume

Not every bulk buyer needs the biggest bundle. If you're flipping a handful of cars a month, the 15-credit bundle at $74.99 ($5.00/report) likely covers you without tying up cash in unused credits. If you're running a full retail lot with steady trade-in volume, the 25-credit bundle's $4.00/report rate is the better long-run cost basis. Frequently asked questions

Do I need a dealer license to buy bulk credits?

No. Bundles are open to anyone — private buyers, flippers, small dealers and exporters all check out the same way, with no dealer license or business registration required.

Is there a subscription or monthly commitment?

No. You buy a bundle once and the credits are yours to redeem on your own schedule. There's no recurring charge and no minimum monthly usage.

Can I run both Carfax and AutoCheck on the same import vehicle?

Yes — spend two credits on one VIN to pull a Carfax report and an AutoCheck report for cross-referenced pre-import due diligence.

What happens if the vehicle has no US history?

A thin or empty report is itself informative for import buyers — it tells you the vehicle wasn't reported to major US databases, which is worth verifying against the paperwork before you commit to shipping.
